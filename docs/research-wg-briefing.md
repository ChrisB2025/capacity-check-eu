# Capacity Check Europe: briefing for the Research working group

November 2026. Prepared for the MML EU Research working group, the first of the review layers agreed on 18 November (then Dirk Ehnts, then outside economists and partner organisations).

## What it is

A low-cost proof of concept for real resource scoring. It applies the same public spending shock in all 27 member states (1% of GDP, spent in one year on energy-efficiency renovation of existing homes), traces it through Eurostat's input-output tables to 64 industries, turns the output needed into jobs, and sets that against how often firms already report labour shortages. Each industry in each country gets an estimated risk category: clear, tight or binding. These are risk indicators for early screening, not forecasts and not verdicts on whether a programme can be delivered.

Labour is the first resource because the data exist, are comparable across the EU, and matter politically. Energy is the proposed second.

## The tool's verdict on the data

The data page carries a verdict generated by the tool from a desk audit of free data on 19 November 2026. It is a machine judgement, not anyone's position, and it is there to be challenged:

> Not unviable. A pan-European screen works at the level of industries and broad sectors: for labour now, and very probably for a programme's energy use and climate footprint next. Below that level (trades and occupations, physical capacity for energy and materials, most ecological limits, and any agreed social floor) comparable EU data runs out, and the method has to be taken up nationally or locally with each country's own data.

## Questions for the working group

1. **Is the verdict right?** Where is the threshold between what a pan-European tool can do and what needs national data?
2. **Is the question right?** Is labour the right first resource, and is the standard shock a fair test?
3. **Where is the method weakest?** In particular the rating thresholds, the spending mix, and the jobs conversion.
4. **What would make you trust or distrust the output?**
5. **Is this a research tool the working group would use, or want to develop?** If so, which next step: materials and equipment shortages (uses data already held), energy use and emissions, energy poverty as social context, or a national deep dive with a partner?
6. **Who should see it next?** Including whether it suits a working partnership with Doughnut Economics Action Lab.

Answers can go through the "For reviewers" page in the tool, which has a feedback form, or in the meeting.
