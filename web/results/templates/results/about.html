Method in brief

  1. Direct demand. A composition table splits the spending across the products bought directly; each is assigned to the industry with the same code. See how the shock is allocated.
  2. Supply chains. Eurostat's FIGARO inter-country input-output tables ({{ industries }} industries, 50 economies) give the output required from each industry in each economy, split into home, rest of EU and outside EU.
  3. Jobs. Persons employed per euro of output, from Eurostat's national accounts.
  4. Pressure. Extra persons as a share of the industry's employment.
  5. Availability. The share of firms naming labour as limiting (DG ECFIN business surveys), placed in its own history.
  6. Estimated risk category. Clear, tight, binding or no rating, from pressure and availability together. Thresholds are parameters, each with a stated reason; the thresholds page shows how the results move with them. Categories are risk indicators, not factual judgements.
  7. Headline. The share of a programme's jobs on tight or binding lines.
  8. Sweden. Swedish jobs split into occupations and compared with official forecasts.
  9. Robustness. Other compositions, thresholds, slack indicator and FIGARO edition.
  10. Plausibility. France and Ireland against official job estimates.

Named limits

  • One shock, 27 different countries. The same financial shock in countries with different climates, building stock, tenure, incomes and habits. Compare relative pressure, not what each country would need to spend or do.
  • Construction is one industry. FIGARO cannot split it into the trades renovation needs; only the Sweden layer can.
  • Jobs are not hires. Firms can reprioritise and households can substitute; a high category signals risk, not certain hiring.
  • Survey shares count firms, not missing workers.
