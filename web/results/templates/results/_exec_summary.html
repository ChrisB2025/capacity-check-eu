What was tested
Extra public spending of {{ sm.shock_pct|floatformat }}% of {{ sm.gdp_year }} GDP on housing and energy renovation, in one year, in {{ sm.countries }} member state{{ sm.countries|pluralize }}{% if sm.countries > 1 %}{% if p.application == "simultaneous" %}, all spending at once{% else %}, each counted on its own{% endif %}{% endif %}: €{{ sm.shock_meur|bn }}bn in all. The spending mix is the same everywhere. The question is whether the industries that would do the work already show signs of labour shortage.
What it found
{{ sm.headline_jobs_weighted|pct }} of the jobs the spending would create fall in industries rated tight or binding, where at least one of the two signals points to labour pressure.{% if sm.countries > 1 %} By member state the share runs from {{ sm.headline_min|pct }} ({{ min_name|default:sm.headline_min_country }}) to {{ sm.headline_max|pct }} ({{ max_name|default:sm.headline_max_country }}), so the headline separates countries only weakly.{% endif %} Construction is tight or binding in {{ sm.construction_tight_or_binding }} of the {{ sm.construction_rated }} member state{{ sm.construction_rated|pluralize }} with a rating, and binding in {{ sm.construction_binding }}. A further {{ sm.unrated_jobs_weighted|pct }} of the jobs fall in industries with no rating, mostly because the business surveys do not cover them.

Where the tool sits
An early screen of real resource risk, before a programme is designed: it shows which industries to look at more closely, and where training or phasing might be needed. It is not a forecast, and not a verdict on whether a programme can be delivered.
